## Mythdirection

If [[20 - 29 Myths/20. Myths\|Myths]] are shortcuts for our brains, what happens when someone changes the off ramp? **Mythdirection** is the process by which an actor leverages expectations associated with Myths and [[20 - 29 Myths/25. Mythoscapes\|Mythoscapes]] to accomplish a different goal than was originally intended. The gap between style and substance can be exploited to provide the appearance of legitimacy and a chance to redirect the underlying functions of norms.

## First, An Illustration

If you've ever flipped on HGTV (or waited in the lobby of a car repair shop), it's likely that you've watched a couple episodes of a *House Hunters*-like reality TV show. If you *really* scrolled through the endless sea of clones on Hulu, there's even a nonzero chance you've come across a show called **[First-Time Buyer](https://web.archive.org/web/20260403103647/http://www.firsttimebuyer.realtor/)**, created by the... *National Association of Realtors*?? Huh???

For as formulaic as reality TV can already be, a particular strain of house-hunting shows share a set of tropes with unmatched prevalence. As prospective buyers (an inoffensive couple offering light banter) search for their new home (each with a lower-third graphic listing the price), their real estate agent must navigate their disagreements (do we stretch our budget or make compromises?). It's banal, yet wildly compelling. What tiny nitpick will prevent Kevin from agreeing with Julie's dream home? Will a Property Brother say something cringe? Are they going to paint over that beautiful, original brick wall? Such a well-defined Mythoscape makes it easy for viewers to jump in to any episode of any show in the subgenre and quickly join in on the fun.

>[!info] ***Nothing Unusual Here...***
>With hundreds of thousands of episodes across these shows, we're also given a relatively clear picture of the "[[30 - 39 Power Weapons/34. Normalization\|normal]]" homebuying experience - one that has no place for housing co-ops, multifamily living, or a number of other valid lifestyles. It's always a picture-perfect family.
>
>Nathan Fielder and Benny Safdie's *The Curse* (2023) does a good job satirizing this, in my opinion, following the drama of a dysfunctional couple with an HGTV show as they gentrify a neighborhood. If you only have 11 minutes, watch Mike Tyson sell a haunted house in *Mike Tyson Mysteries*, S1E8 (2014).

House-hunting shows also operate within the broader ruleset of reality TV. Because the audience generally knows shows are "fake" or influenced in some way, the show's presentation gets away without explicit disclaimer, and enough variables come from the real (*well, hired*) people and real (*well, staged*) scenarios to maintain some spontaneity. While lacking in fantastical scenery or crafted dialogue like a traditionally scripted show, viewers watch knowing that exciting events *just outside* the mundane are always around the corner. This means the blurry lines of reality are always looming in the background. 

*Did that actually happen? Are people really like this?*

If this doesn't seem like there's potential for something sinister yet, let's compare it to the kayfabe of professional wrestling. Reality TV shares some initial resemblance, but consider where the guardrails have been taken down. At least in professional wrestling, the audience knows...
- The people on stage are playing characters or a heightened version of themselves
- This is an event put on specifically to entertain a crowd
- Actions are not necessarily meant to be imitated

Now take the average house-hunting show. Sure, we vaguely understand there's a level of fiction even if just for the simple fact of cameras being pointed at humans. There might be some self-censoring, some more makeup than usual, or a producer-fed line here and there just to make it presentable for national television. All of that is to be expected, and it's balanced by the knowledge that we are watching a couple genuinely trying to go through the homebuying process. This adds a new wrinkle: the show presents as entertainment *and* informational. Viewers can feasibly self-insert to this homebuying process, so now the validity of real-world details are on the table. But the nature of a comfort (or even background noise) show calls for the audience to suspend disbelief for minor details rather than fact check the housing market in Charlotte. 

Here's the thing: **ambiguity makes particularly ripe channels for Mythdirection.** 

The National Association of Realtors has an agenda around the interests of their members. Realtors are generally paid through sales commissions, so a national market with a higher number of home sales would be beneficial to them. With their *First-Time Buyer* show leveraging this house-hunting Mythoscape, how easily can they sell their preferred policies without anyone even realizing they're currently in the market? What disputed knowledge will be taken for granted as fact because it wasn't evident someone was making an argument for it? You be the judge!!
