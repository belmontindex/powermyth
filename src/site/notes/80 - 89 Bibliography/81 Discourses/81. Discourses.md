# Plemorism, the Return to Fullness

Include the basics of the system here.

My intention when developing this framework was so that it could be a means to understanding social inequity. But if so, a reader may reasonably ask what is the whole purpose of all this nonsense? Why are we even approaching it this way, with all these different categories and names? It should seem relatively obvious: someone who is homeless has no money, and a billionaire has a lot of money. That's all you need to know.
- Yet it isn't that simple in practice. Beliefs of homeless vs. billionaires. Social and health issues, drugs, housing markets, historic racial discrimination, a million other things.
- These issues are seemingly unrelated but are actually inextricably linked.

As Du Bois advised 100 years ago, removing ==who the problems manifest in== is not enough when the complexities that bring problems forward will just manifest elsewhere.[^2] Mainstream academic traditions that explore social conflict do not equip us with the tools needed to understand, and address, complex social configurations. The classic base-superstructure model that drives orthodox Marxism's emphasis on class relations fails to account for the mutual interdetermination of culture on economic life.[^1] Contemporary contributions, from fields like Critical Race Theory, DisCrit, etc. all provide exceedingly valuable reference points to marginalized identities, but they lack a throughline that informs some universal application. Both are incredibly useful, of course, and hugely informative to this approach. However, their exclusive focuses have not prepared us for a digital society. What is economic domination when assets are so abstract? What is a marginalized identity when mingling on forums could be with either an anonymous human or a robot?

If this era is one of connection - with instantaneous, global exchange of information - perhaps it would be better served with a model using a base of Western Marxism. Though the scholars that make up this loosely defined tradition did not necessarily have intellectual conformity (Jay, p. 10), they shared an understanding that the reality of the total process was more real than isolated facts (Jay, p. 122). This space provides a number of analytic tools: hegemony, from Gramsci; reification, from Lukacs; technological rationalization from Marcuse, and so on. While this is a step to understanding new forms of domination in the modern world, it is still incomplete. Their description of factors preventing change offered few methods of potential action. Criticized for overly abstract concepts and written in inaccessible language. ==another thing or two==

Basically, no tradition (or perspective) has a complete answer, but our goal is to create suggestions to see how the pieces interrelate. As a result, Pleromism pulls from a diverse set of intellectual traditions. Sociology, STS, post-structuralism, post-modernism, and cultural studies primarily. Pleromic Analysis also pulls from different methodological approaches, discourse analysis, genealogy, critical constructivism, etc.

# Power
*See also: [[80 - 89 Bibliography/81 Discourses/81.1. Power Discourse\|81.1. Power Discourse]]*

The three dimensions of power is helpful to understand [[10 - 19 Power/12. Negotiators\|12. Negotiators]], but what folks missed here is that Power is not all about domination and is also not just about intention. It is helpful to understand *actions* of power's mechanisms, but not necessarily the construct as a whole.

For that, we must turn to the context that enables such actions. Bourdieu's types of capital help us understand [[10 - 19 Power/11. Capacitors\|11. Capacitors]], or what might accumulate so that individuals can express its mechanisms.

Bourdieu and Foucault then articulate how power embedded in social structures (culture and institutions) are eternally sort of reproduced, coming off as [[10 - 19 Power/13. Emitters\|13. Emitters]] through doxa or policy or whatever else.

However, Hayward critiques Foucault because power was too diffuse, so there needs to be some way to make it actionable. That's where her definition, the network of social boundaries that shape what is or isn't possible, comes in.

We can add in Latour here, with ANT. Each entity is relevant here, that's what the [[10 - 19 Power/14. Conduits\|14. Conduits]] are. They have different relationships, different properties, that all play their part with different interests.

# Myth

We know people have different configurations, and some of it is imperceptible because it's individual psychology... but what we can tell is that there are some common structures that people are at least responding to - myth.

Mostly sociology, structuralism I guess? From Levi-Strauss to Roland Barthes (Mythologies, where myth is a second-order sign system that takes a historical meaning and presents it as natural. Myth *is* ideology), Fredric Jameson (The Political Unconscious, where myths are "symbolic resolutions" of real social contradictions), and Althusser (ideology as an imaginary relation to real conditions)
- **Laclau and Mouffe use "myth" and "social imaginary" for narratives that suture a dislocated social order**
