## Free to Change: The Power/Myth Blueprint

>[!tip] **CONGRATULATIONS!** You found a page you weren't supposed to see yet!
>Last updated July 17, 2026

### Introduction

This ultimate project intends to weave together the key works from the [[01. Overview\|PowerMyth Index]] in a 7-part narrative. There are three overarching goals:
1) Outline its theoretical framework through case studies and comparative historical analysis;
2) Demonstrate how Power and oppression manifest in Cyberspace; and
3) Contribute original research on social inequity through a study of digital platform governance.

Each part should be accessible to someone who has no prior knowledge of others. Above all, hopefully it strikes the right balance between academic rigor and good storytelling!!! 

### [[zz Book/00-1. Power-Renaissance\|00-1. Power-Renaissance]]

**What is Power?** *How does Power change as a result of Cyberspace, and why can no one agree on basic facts?*

This section defines [[10 - 19 Power/10. Power\|Power]] and its centrality to addressing social inequity. While much policy focus is given to disparities in wealth, there are a number of other [[10 - 19 Power/14. Power Capacitors\|Power Capacitors]] (social, informational, cultural, and symbolic) that accumulate like capital and serve as potential enablers for entities to exert influence. While this section will provide a brief overview of the full PowerMyth framework (p. 10 - 15), it will generally stay rooted on the familiar and tangible weaponization of Power through [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/35. Concentration\|Concentration]].

Due to the corresponding [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/73. Weapons_C/73.05. Prevalence\|Centralization]] of [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/70. Cyberspace\|Cyberspace]]'s infrastructure, *information* will emerge as the most important Capacitor for today's age. This will bring us to consider the [[20 - 29 Myths/28 World Mythoscapes/28.01. Knowledge\|Knowledge]] Mythoscape, or the assumed processes by which we gather information and reason what is or is not "true." The section will end with the relationship between those who control information empires and the [[60 - 69 Power Generators/61 Heterodoxy/61.00. Heterodoxy\|Heterodoxy]] of the esoteric traditions that defy them.

### [[zz Book/00-2. Dominant-Reality\|00-2. Dominant-Reality]]

**How are individuals and society shaped?** *Who gets a say, and what happens when only experts understand the rules behind it all?*

This section defines [[10 - 19 Power/11. Realms of Freedom\|Realms of Freedom]], or the perceptions each person has about what is good, rational, or possible. In society, the [[10 - 19 Power/12. Dominant Realm\|Dominant Realm]] does not reconcile the contradictions of all individual Realms, leaving many nonconformists at a structural disadvantage. There is an eternal process of [[10 - 19 Power/15. Boundary Negotiation\|Boundary Negotiation]] affecting this, but the [[10 - 19 Power/13. Realm Enforcers\|Realm Enforcers]] of culture and institutions provide more avenues than not for the maintenance of the status quo.

Because unstated knowledge and/or passive continuation is the primary mechanism of the Dominant Realm, this section will explore Power weaponized as [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/34. Normalization\|Normalization]] through the contrast of [[60 - 69 Power Generators/61 Heterodoxy/61.01. Countercultures\|Countercultures]] and the technocracy. The authenticity sought by counterculturalists and the hyper-rationalization of computer technology will come to a head in early Cyberspace, as we explore the development of Cyberculture. This section will end with a consideration of the new technocracy, where [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/73. Weapons_C/73.06. Reliance\|Reliance]] on technology has turned supposed tools for self-sufficiency into everyday decisions offloaded to billion dollar corporations.

### [[zz Book/00-3. Myth-Time\|00-3. Myth-Time]]

**How do stories shape freedom?** *What even is reality, and how does its translation to virtuality redefine the norms of the outside world?*

This section defines [[20 - 29 Myths/20. Myths\|Myths]], the stories we tell to rationalize the world we live in. Common genres persist and adapt with time, such as [[20 - 29 Myths/21 The Villain/21.00. The Villain\|The Villain]] appearing nearly identically as the heretical [[60 - 69 Power Generators/61 Heterodoxy/61.02. Gnosticism\|Gnostics]] of the 1st century AD and the Communists 2000 years later. The truth of Myths is relative, however; all stories are simplified accounts from a certain perspective, anyway. Because of this, we also need to define [[20 - 29 Myths/25. Mythoscapes\|Mythoscapes]], the networks of Myths that inform identity and culture. Mythoscapes vary in terms of malleability and purpose of subscription, though their collective nature ensures a corresponding Dominant Realm.

Both Myths and Mythoscapes can be objects of [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/31 Appropriation/31.00. Appropriation\|Appropriation]], which is one of the most dangerous weapons of Power due to its subtlety. Whether knowingly or not, entities can appeal to a Mythoscape in order to buy enough goodwill for significant change toward their own interest. This is particularly relevant to [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/73. Weapons_C/73.01. Appropriation_C\|Cyberspace]], where unwritten rules about [[20 - 29 Myths/28 World Mythoscapes/28.01. Knowledge\|Knowledge]], [[20 - 29 Myths/28 World Mythoscapes/28.03. Connection\|Connection]], [[20 - 29 Myths/28 World Mythoscapes/28.04. Celebrity\|Celebrity]], and other universal systems of social participation no longer operate as they did in [[20 - 29 Myths/28 World Mythoscapes/28.05. Reality\|Reality]]. Instead of being collectively adjusted though, they have been rewritten by those who maintain digital infrastructure to encourage profit at the expense of harmony.

### [[zz Book/00-4. Law-Space\|00-4. Law-Space]]

**What facilitates social change?** *How do different institutions interact with Power, and what hope do we have for equity in today's world?*

This section defines [[40 - 49 Power Conduits/40. Power Conduits\|Power Conduits]], the entities targeted for Dominant Realm contraction or expansion. Because culture and institutions have a highly related, cyclical relationship, effective Boundary Negotiation must consider the unique properties of [[40 - 49 Power Conduits/41. Government\|Government]], [[40 - 49 Power Conduits/42. Academia\|Academia]], [[40 - 49 Power Conduits/43. Religion\|Religion]], [[40 - 49 Power Conduits/44. Media\|Media]], and so on. Each opens pathways for change; simultaneously, each can be vessels for [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/30. Power Weapons\|Power Weapons]] if not properly scrutinized. Unfortunately for us, the severity of *Cyberspace* Conduits hasn't yet been fully grappled with by the public.

**This section is the climax of the project and contains the original research contribution.** For however inaccessible Power Conduits generally are in the outside world, little else could be as closed off as the rules for [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/74. Conduits_C/74.01. Platforms\|Digital Platforms]], where social interaction in Cyberspace occurs, or the [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/74. Conduits_C/74.04. Algorithms\|Ranking Algorithms]] that determine what content is or is not seen on them. As Cyberspace's self-governed communities became increasingly governed by tech empires, it is likely that fundamental aspects of our culture have changed without so much as an attempt at government regulation. A Discourse Analysis of 1990s Usenet forums compared to Reddit posts today should help illuminate this. When so much of everyday communication occurs on Cyberspace, what does it mean for society when its infrastructure is built for the interests of corporations?

### [[zz Book/00-5. Art-Shmups\|00-5. Art-Shmups]]

**Power isn't going anywhere... what now?** *Power is embedded in the unlikeliest places, from the invisible to the assumed, so how can we avoid fatalism and fight for the future?*

This section, the turning point of the project, will slow to reflect on the fluid nature of Power's mechanisms. For example, where [[50 - 59 Power Tools/52. Art\|Art]] is often a Power Tool that humanizes other perspectives through vulnerable expressions of self, the same medium can be a Weapon to profit off a derivative product that reinforces narratives of the Dominant Realm. [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/33. Categorization\|Categorization]] can be a dehumanizing method of organizing groups of people, or it can sort through large datasets for greater human efficiency. And as we've seen, [[50 - 59 Power Tools/54. Technology\|Technology]] can be used to increase quality of life just as it can increase ease of oppression. 

As a result, we will consider multiple areas where potential gifts are merely wolves in disguise. First, how online application of [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/71. Power_C/71.03a. Copyright\|Copyright]] laws promised creative control and ownership but resulted in the [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/37. Repression\|Repression]] of Dominant Realm critique. Second, how [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/73. Weapons_C/73.03. Surveillance\|Surveillance]] crept in under the guise of convenience and personalization and threatens anonymity and freedom. Lastly, how [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/32 Echocultures/32.00. Echocultures\|Echocultures]] like the [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/32 Echocultures/32.01. Alt-Right\|Alt-Right]], the [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/32 Echocultures/32.02. Manosphere\|Manosphere]], and [[30 - 39 Power Weapons/32 Echocultures/32.03. Conspiracists\|Conspiracy Theorists]] mirrored countercultures with anti-establishment rhetoric despite advocating for a previous, more contracted, Dominant Realm. The section will conclude with an attempt to outline a consistent method of distinguishing between "good" and "bad" actions.

### [[zz Book/00-6. Tools-Revival\|00-6. Tools-Revival]]

This section defines [[60 - 69 Power Generators/60. Power Generators\|Power Generators]], the oppressed or subaltern found outside the Dominant Realm. Their stories are often untold, turned into a spectacle, or otherwise mythologized by those excluding them in the first place. Perhaps this has fostered an exceptional ability to develop [[50 - 59 Power Tools/50. Power Tools\|Power Tools]], which means we can learn from their [[50 - 59 Power Tools/53. Community\|Solidarity]] and other methods of thriving within the status quo.

Oh wait... wasn't early Cyberculture dominated by Generators before it was subsumed by capitalism? Maybe it's not just a nightmare hellscape after all!! Here, we will discuss the potential of Cyberspace through [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/76. Generators_C/76.03. Hackers\|Hacker]] mavericks, digital utopianism and tools to getting there, the realities of the modern [[70 - 79 Cyberspace/76. Generators_C/76.02. Web Revival\|Web Revival]] efforts, and more.

### [[zz Book/00-7. Freedom-Genesis\|00-7. Freedom-Genesis]]

This project closes by defining the ultimate paradox for societies: **GOVERNANCE**. If we are to eliminate social inequity, how do we factor in so many different Realms or mitigate the impact of all these Weapons? This outline is admittedly pretty loose so far, but in all likelihood, we will talk about the successes and contradictions of the [[60 - 69 Power Generators/63 Nation/63.02. American Revolution\|American Revolution]], alongside the mythology that surrounds it to this day. We will consider the Haudenosaunee/Iroquois Confederacy and the many other groups that the Framers pulled from to circle back on the idea of Appropriation.

The basic idea is that this section will serve as a series of recommendations for where to take Cyberspace next. I think I'll pull partly from my experiences attempting to restructure our regional homeless system governance, as it is a great microcosm for a lot of the overly-bureaucratic, hyper-specialized, and agenda-conflicting hurdles prevalent in modern spaces. And... yeah, I dunno. This is where we wrap it all up! Hooray!!!!
