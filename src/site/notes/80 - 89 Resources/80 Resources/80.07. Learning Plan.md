This page outlines the key fields of study that I think could be relevant to my dissertation. My current progress with each varies, so each category focuses mostly on anticipated next steps. This page was developed in conjunction with the full [[80 - 89 Resources/80 Resources/80.05. ## Theory

#### Western Marxism
**Importance:** ★★★★★
Critical analysis of the culture industry, ideology, and hegemony are essential to understanding current work in Critical Digital Studies. They're also ripe for helping me develop my own ideas! Marcuse and Gramsci have already been helpful for this, and I figure I should *at least* be versed in the basics of Lukács/reification, Adorno & Horkheimer stuff generally (*Dialectic of Enlightenment* and *The Authoritarian Personality*), Habermas, Benjamin... and probably even Sartre/Lefebvre existentialist stuff?? I dunno, everyone seems extremely relevant.

**Comfort:** ★★☆☆☆
I will seek out guidance here. I know the big names and have read some amount already; however, I really have no clue where to start with some of them. Althusser? No idea. Since there's such a high volume of work here, I'd need to sort through the landscape before diving in.

#### Sociology
**Importance:** ★★★★★
Dominant culture, culture formation, resistance, mechanisms of repression, etc... Bourdieu, of course. Stuart Hall and the Birmingham School's Cultural Studies are hugely important here. So are predecessors like the Chicago School and DuBois/the Atlanta School. Countercultural studies (sometimes). Digital Sociology is still emerging, though some really great stuff exists there.

**Comfort:** ★★★★☆
The tricky part is accidentally spending time on ethnographic work that reinforces dominant culture rather than examining power structures. Generally speaking, I think I've found the niches I need.

#### Postmodern Philosophy
**Importance:** ★★★★☆
The nature of reality and truth are absolutely essential to understanding Cyberspace... and a lot of where I've come to thus far either dances around this area or independently aligned with it. Time for formal exploration!! Key topics of interest might be...
- Poststructuralism/instability of meaning (Derrida, Deleuze)
- Simulacra & hyperreality (Baudrillard)
- Metanarratives (Lyotard)
- Identity & performativity (Judith Butler?)
- Subaltern studies (Said/*Orientalism*?)
- More Foucault of course...

**Comfort:** ★★☆☆☆
I will seek out guidance here. Similar to Western Marxism above, I know the big names and general direction, but this stuff is so complicated that it'd be helpful to talk concepts through with other people.

#### Science and Technology Studies
**Importance:** ★★★★☆
The intersection of philosophy and cyberspace. Pretty inexperienced here, but it seems like a super obvious next step!! Key topics of interest might be...
- Actor-Network Theory (Latour, *Science in Action*)
- Infrastructure Studies (Susan Leigh Star?)
- Social Construction of Technology (Trevor Pinch & Wiebe Bijker?)
- Technological determinism and social shaping
- Scientific controversy/consensus studies
- Boundary work between "science" and "non-science"
- Co-production of knowledge and social order

**Comfort:** ★★☆☆☆
I will seek out guidance here, starting with Cutcher Gershenfeld. Likely a field with immense relevance, so it's necessary to narrow down exactly what will be the most help to my study first.

#### Classical Marxism
**Importance:** ★★★☆☆
Obviously foundational in many ways re: class relations/domination, but since my research is less focused on material disparities, I think I have most of what I need to know here for now?

**Comfort:** ★★★★☆
Key areas that might be worth exploring further include revolution/organizing in practice, perspectives from Latin America, modern Classical Marxist critiques of Western Marxism, etc.

## Policy & Application

#### Cyberspace
**Importance:** ★★★★★
The most important area to become an expert in... but it's also the trickiest since it's spread out across a million different academic **and** non-academic spaces. Key areas include platform/algorithmic governance and cyberculture/virtual norm development. It will also be necessary to understand what's going on with those leading independent or open-source Cyberspace infrastructure, as well as Web3 (e.g., Blockchain, Crypto, DAOs).

**Comfort:** ????
I have a huge backlog of things to dive into... from people (Lessig, Wu, Zuboff, Gillespie, all the folks through the Web Revival, etc.) to organizations (IGF, GigaNet, ICANN, Metagovernance, RadicalXChange, etc.). So we'll see where it all leads once I dip my toes in a little bit more.

#### Governance
**Importance:** ★★★★☆
Informs possible policy interventions for the dissertation. May or may not be directly translatable to Cyberspace, but likely to work in tandem with platform governance.

**Comfort:** ★★★☆☆
I have some personal and academic experience from the public administration perspective (i.e., navigating and maintaining organizational structures), but it would be really nice to have a primer on alternative models of collective governance (maybe looking at what goes wrong and right with communes, worker cooperatives, CBOs, governing boards for MSIs, etc.).

#### Critical Race Theory
**Importance:** ★★★☆☆
Home to many modern Marxists. Excellent beacon for understanding intersectionality and inequity reproduced by both institutions and culture. Was an important area as my introduction to many of the ideas explored here, though might not be something to go further with at this point.

**Comfort:** ★★★★☆
Have spent lots of academic time here, so I might know where/what I can reach back into without much more guidance. Definitely would like more time with Crenshaw and Bonilla-Silva. Could also use time with Critical Whiteness Studies.

#### Poverty & Homelessness
**Importance:** ★★★☆☆
Obviously focused more on the material/economic, but has more actual policy to look at... plus, the psychology and culture of domination come up pretty often here anyway.

**Comfort:** ★★★★★
Super comfortable here, nothing much to say.

#### Community Development(?)
**Importance:** ★★☆☆☆
Not sure if there's a specific field on this, but thinking of policy work that contributes to social/community growth, economic solidarity, etc. Bowling Alone-type stuff, I guess. Likely more relevant for the Blueprint than the dissertation itself.

**Comfort:** ★★☆☆☆
I have a rough idea here but would need to seek guidance if I pursued it. Could probably find a lot through community organizers of justice or inequality groups.

## Miscellaneous

#### Research Methods
**Importance:** ★★★★★
Necessary to conduct original research :)

**Comfort:** ★★★★★
I have taken many quantitative methods courses and learned of the philosophy behind research and knowledge production. In Fall 2026, I will be taking an overview of qualitative methods and a course on Discourse Analysis (which will be my likely dissertation method). I think we're fine here.

#### Western Esotericism
**Importance:** ★★★☆☆
Super helpful for understanding metaphysics, rejected knowledge, and the relationship between dominant culture and those outside of it. Influential to Western Marxism and Cultural Studies.

**Comfort:** ★★★☆☆
A course in this field would be incredible, to be honest... but that's pretty unlikely due to obscurity. I think I have a good enough grip on resources at my disposal to get what I need independently.

#### History (various)
**Importance:** ★★★☆☆
Helpful to see how others told stories, helpful for my own storytelling, helpful for examples and general context... and on and on and on.

**Comfort:** ★★★★★
I don't think I'll have room to *seek out* any courses, but coming across one in an area of interest (American Revolution, Civil Rights, ancient civilizations, educational/cultural renaissances) would be super convenient. Self-directed learning here is generally straightforward enough, though.

#### Theology (various)
**Importance:** ★★☆☆☆
Could be helpful to learn more about the church's unique role in cultural production and how that has evolved throughout history. How is authority established, how do modern institutions mimic it?

**Comfort:** ★☆☆☆☆
I've never taken a course in this field and have limited experience with it. It would be nice if I could find a course very specifically related to research area, but it's a lower priority since I anticipate coming across it in philosophy already.
