## Pleromism

**Pleromism aims toward an integrated theory and method to use for research on inequity oriented toward totality.**

We simultaneously face an impending climate disaster, an extreme concentration of wealth contrasted by a growing population sleeping outside, and technology that evolves at a faster rate than we can reasonably control.[^1] These issues are enormously complex and woven into the fabric of everyday life that stretches across competing economic, political, and cultural interests. Some of those decisive factors are visible, like actors and their material resources. Others, like policies and the abstract ideas behind them, are harder to observe. Regardless, each one is a potential barrier to redirecting the trajectory of major social issues.

So, is change possible? Or are we simply prisoners to the wills of our ancestors and kings, destined toward some catastrophic fate? The answer can only be revealed if we systematically chart out the components related to two processes:
- **What is the social order and how is it shaped?**
- **What enables or prevents change?**

An equitable society is one in which individuals and groups have reasonable capacity to reshape their own boundaries of what is or is not possible. Fostering a social order that reflects the will of its people is a tall order, though it begins with unmasking mechanisms of power. Only when there is a unified recognition of how seemingly unrelated aspects of society are interconnected can we achieve **PLEROMA**.

# Order

In social life, there are a given set of boundaries that define what is good, possible, rational, normal, and so on. These boundaries constitute a **[[30 - 39 Realms/30. Realms\|REALM]]**.

![realms define good, legal, normal](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/Realm.gif)

Boundaries may be explicit or implicit. Regardless, we know these boundaries to exist because when individuals act outside of them, they do so at their expense: If someone breaks the law, they are punished; if someone acts weird or unethically, there are social consequences. This is the nature of the social contract.

Consequences might be more visible in a collective, but they can also be self-imposed. Therefore, it is important to recognize that REALMS exist at scale. Each individual has their own sense of good and rational, and this may or may not completely align with that of the collective. Forming the collective REALM of society, then, means some of the contradicting interests that naturally occur in a group are either reconciled or excluded.

![collective realms exclude necessarily](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/collective2.gif)

These boundaries are perpetually scrutinized to more closely reflect the general will of the collective. Attempts to negotiate them target various **[[10 - 19 Power/14. Conduits\|CONDUITS]]** in society, which convey and uphold the boundaries at any given point. CONDUITS are commonly cultural expressions or institutional entities: Congress, language, art, family, religion, media, science, and any other construct that plays a role distinguishing between what is inside or outside the REALM.

CONDUITS (much like the individual REALMS that form the collective) are not perfectly in agreement; that is to be expected. They cannot be understood in isolation, though. They influence one another and co-produce the processes that inform daily life, and the interrelated network is the social order.

![conduits absorb mechanics of power](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/conduits2.gif)

The dominant configuration of CONDUITS at any given time, or the positions of these elements that make up the complex whole of society, is our main focus of inquiry.

In an equitable society, such a configuration would most closely represent the interests of all individuals who are a part. Were boundaries democratically drawn by all members of a collective REALM, and did the previously described reconciliation and exclusion occur under mutual terms? Or were certain subsets of the population denied the right to vote? Were beliefs reproduced without intention when implicitly acquired at a young age? Without fair opportunity for all to knowingly shape the boundaries that constrain them, domination occurs.

# Change

If the dominant configuration of CONDUITS did not simply occur out of mutual agreement between parties involved in a collective, what determined their position? To answer this, we must turn to **[[10 - 19 Power/10. Power\|POWER]]** and its many manifestations.

![power defines what's possible](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/power_full.gif)

Certain properties offer the *potential* to reshape the boundaries of possibility. Wealth, status, and information are a few subtypes of POWER **[[10 - 19 Power/11. Capacitors\|CAPACITORS]]** that can be accumulated to determine the influence and autonomy of actors. As is the case with capital, CAPACITORS can accumulate negatively as well as positively.

![capacitors change to tools](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/type1.gif)

Then, that potential can be converted into active expressions of POWER. **[[10 - 19 Power/12. Negotiators\|NEGOTIATORS]]** represent the mechanisms that expand or contract the boundaries of possibility, e.g., passing a new law, paying off a debt, or creating an artistic expression.

There is a variable level of impact from NEGOTIATORS, where reshaping collective boundaries would usually be a result of targeting CONDUITS. Given the diversity of individual REALMS in a collective, it is often the case that these mechanisms do not have universal application (what expands in one direction may require contraction in another).

![power shifts the boundaries](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/negotiators2.gif)

Lastly, in the event that mechanisms of POWER are embedded in CONDUITS, they are perpetually reflected back to the collective as **[[10 - 19 Power/13. Emitters\|EMITTERS]]**. Passive effects from cultures and institutions, such as laws, norms, and customs, reproduce the actions that shaped them.

![cultures and institutions maintain boundaries](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/emitters.gif)

If a discriminatory law continually restricts access to CAPACITORS to those who would otherwise change it, or if social expectations ostracize those who think differently... existing configurations can be reinforced indefinitely. 

# Limits

So, we are subjects of a world where what we can or cannot do is defined by the meanings embedded in a network of the cultures and institutions that we are born into, which are in some ways outside of our immediate control. Yet simultaneously, we were the agents responsible for shaping this network in the first place! Why do we not then pursue a more equitable collective REALM? There are two primary reasons.

First, we must consider how collective REALMS, which are imperfectly composed of individual REALMS, are a macrocosm for the imperfect composition of individual REALMS. Every individual is ascribed, or subscribes to, a dynamic set of relationally defined fields of knowledge and behaviors called **[[20 - 29 Myths/25. Mythoscapes\|MYTHOSCAPES]]**. An individual's identities (race, class, gender, age, etc.) and affiliations (region, occupation, political party, etc.) may not completely overlap but set baselines for what actions and thoughts are defined in response to.

(==GIF HERE==)

MYTHOSCAPES develop their own set of **[[20 - 29 Myths/20. Myths\|MYTHS]]** to rationalize the world and reconcile contradictions in normative structures. These stories typically adapt common structures to outline who should be disliked ([[20 - 29 Myths/21 The Villain/21. The Villain\|The Villain]]) and who should be rooted for ([[20 - 29 Myths/22 The Hero/22. The Hero\|The Hero]]). They may also set the stage ([[20 - 29 Myths/23 The Setting/23. The Setting\|The Setting]]) and describe what the heroes are up against ([[20 - 29 Myths/24 The Conflict/24. The Conflict\|The Conflict]]). Validity is irrelevant; what matters is how MYTHS provide socially constructed meaning to the group they are bonded to.

![myths explain our lives](https://dillonbelmont.com/03powermyth-assets/20/mythscroll16.gif)

Second, there is a limit to the scope of social change in a collective borne out of the function of ensuring its stability. Excluded components have incentive to expand the boundaries, but constant negotiations would threaten the REALM'S composition. Therefore, social processes that are typically invisible or otherwise taken for granted will manifest. These are the **[[40 - 49 Realm Enforcers/40. Realm Enforcers\|REALM ENFORCERS]]**:

- **[[40 - 49 Realm Enforcers/41. Monadus\|Monadus]], the leader** who defines the dominant position.
- **[[40 - 49 Realm Enforcers/42. Mithrot\|Mithrot]], the storyteller** who imbues meaning in myths.
- **[[40 - 49 Realm Enforcers/43. Categorius\|Categorius]], the evaluator** who classifies information.
- **[[40 - 49 Realm Enforcers/44. Rationaliza\|Rationaliza]], the peacemaker** who justifies the way it is.
- **[[40 - 49 Realm Enforcers/45. Distributon\|Distributon]], the decider** who allocates resources.
- **[[40 - 49 Realm Enforcers/46. Repressio\|Repressio]], the silencer** who prevents what is unacceptable.
